Xin Di, PhD

Research Assistant Professor · Department of Biomedical Engineering · New Jersey Institute of Technology

I study how the brain works as a network: how regions interact during movies and tasks, across development, and in autism. My work combines fMRI, eye tracking and PET, and develops open methods for measuring connectivity reliably.

Research focus

Naturalistic connectivity & autism

Movie watching and eye tracking reveal how brain networks develop and how they differ across individuals with autism and ADHD.

Methods for connectivity

Task-based connectivity (PPI, beta series), flexible hemodynamic modeling, and open preprocessing and quality-control protocols.

Molecular & metabolic connectivity

PET-based connectivity of glucose metabolism and neurotransmission, and shared data standards for molecular imaging.

Current projects

NJ ACTS · 2026–2027

Standardized data access for molecular imaging

Establishing a Standardized Data Access Protocol (SDAP) to resolve data fragmentation in the US molecular imaging ecosystem.

NJDOH · 2024–2026

Eye movements and brain activity in autism

Validating deep neural network models that extract eye-gaze information directly from fMRI data, then testing how gaze and brain activity differ in autism spectrum disorder.
